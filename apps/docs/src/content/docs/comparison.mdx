Nembrix isn't trying to be every database client for every engine. It's a
**Postgres-first, session-oriented** client built for people who keep several
connections open while shipping a migration. This page is an honest look at
where it fits next to the tools you're probably already using.

:::note
Comparisons reflect each tool's general positioning at the time of writing, not
a feature-by-feature audit of the newest release of every competitor. If
something here is out of date, [open an
issue](https://github.com/oesukam/nembrix/issues).
:::

## The one-liners

**vs. TablePlus** — TablePlus is a polished, broad multi-engine client, but
it's proprietary and its free tier caps open tabs and rows, with several
features (schema diff, some export paths) behind the paid license. Nembrix is
free and open source, goes deeper on Postgres specifically, and treats
**multiple live sessions per saved connection** as a first-class rail rather
than just more tabs.

**vs. DBeaver** — DBeaver is the Swiss-army knife: it connects to nearly
everything. That breadth is also its cost — it's a Java/Eclipse app that can
feel heavy, and the polished bits sit in the paid Pro tier. Nembrix is a
lightweight native app (Tauri/Rust) that trades universal engine support for a
faster, more focused Postgres experience.

**vs. DataGrip** — DataGrip is excellent and IDE-grade, with the best SQL
intelligence in the list — but it's a paid JetBrains subscription and carries
full-IDE weight. If you live in JetBrains already, DataGrip is a natural fit.
Nembrix is the answer when you want a fast, standalone, free client rather than
another IDE window.

**vs. Postico / Sequel Ace** — These are beloved Mac-native clients (Postico
for Postgres, Sequel Ace for MySQL). Nembrix shares their native feel and
Postgres focus, but adds **cross-platform** builds (Windows/Linux too), a
first-class multi-session rail, a heat-mapped `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` viewer, schema
diff, and copy-between-connections — and it's open source.

## When Nembrix is the wrong tool

Being honest cuts both ways. Reach for something else when:

- **You need engines beyond Postgres.** Nembrix is Postgres-first. For MySQL,
  SQL Server, Oracle, Mongo, and friends in one window, DBeaver or DataGrip
  fit better today.
- **You want deep IDE-grade SQL refactoring / code intelligence.** DataGrip
  leads here.
- **You're on a workflow already centered on one of these.** If TablePlus or
  Postico already does everything you need, there's no need to switch.

## Where Nembrix wins

- **Multi-session rail** — several live sessions per saved connection, drag to
  reorder, recents one click away. See [Multiple sessions](/sessions/).
- **Real `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`** — a heat-colored plan tree that surfaces hot
  branches at a glance. See [Query analysis](/analysis/).
- **Copy between connections** — move a table or a whole database between two
  live connections with FK ordering handled for you. See
  [Copy between connections](/copy-between-connections/).
- **Free & open source** — no license tiers, no row caps, no telemetry by
  default.
